## Key / capo

- **Cifra Club labels the song “Tono: Cm”** but the **early verses are written with D, A, G, Dm** (sounds/reads as **D major** for verses 1–2).
- **Later verses modulate up** into **Eb-area chords** (Bb, Eb, Ab, Cm, Fm, Bbm) — a classic half-step-up key change.
- **Capo:** none listed on the only available chart. Play open in the shapes below; if the recording is hard to sing, try capo 1–2 and keep the same shapes (or transpose down later).

**Disagreement to note:** Cifra’s key label (Cm) does not match the D/A/G shapes in verses 1–2. Trust the chord letters for those verses; treat “Cm” as possibly referring to the later section or a site default. With only one chart, this could not be cross-checked against a second chord site.

## Strumming

**Not published** on the chord sites found. For a 1960s march/folk vocal:

1. **Week-1 starter (recommended):** all **downstrokes**, one per beat: `D  D  D  D` (steady march).
2. **Next step:** `D-D-U-U-D-U` (same pattern many people use on *Ballad of the Green Berets*).
3. Mute lightly with the palm if it feels too bright; keep volume even.

Listen to the recording once with the guitar in lap and tap the beat before adding chords.

## Beginner practice plan — chord switching (Week 1)

**Goal:** smooth switches among **D, A, G, Dm** only. Don’t worry about singing the whole song yet.

### Finger tips (shapes)
- **D:** keep a small triangle; thumb behind the neck roughly opposite fret 2; leave the two low strings muted (don’t strum them hard).
- **A:** try the “three-finger row” first; if that’s slow, collapse to a mini-barre with one finger across D/G/B at fret 2.
- **G:** plant middle (low E/3) and index (A/2) first, then add the high-E finger; don’t chase a perfect ring on every string yet — clear D/G/B is enough.
- **Dm:** same neighborhood as D — only the high-E finger slides from fret 2 → 1. Practice **D → Dm** as a “one-finger move.”

### One-minute changes (drill list)
Do each pair for **60 seconds**: form chord → strum once → switch → strum once → repeat. Count clean switches; aim to beat yesterday’s number.

1. **D ↔ A** (most common)
2. **D ↔ G**
3. **A ↔ G**
4. **D ↔ Dm** (tiny move — confidence builder)
5. **Dm ↔ G**
6. **G → D → A → D** (mini song loop)

### Simple strum before the “real” pattern
- Days 1–3: **one downstroke per chord** while changing.
- Days 4–5: **four downstrokes per chord** (one bar each).
- Days 6–7: try `D-D-U-U-D-U` on a single chord for a minute, then on **D–A–D**.

### ~15 min/day routine (Week 1)

| Minutes | What |
|--------:|------|
| 2 | Warm-up: fretting-hand stretch + four clean strums on open strings |
| 5 | One-minute changes (pick 5 pairs from the list above) |
| 4 | Verse-1 loop slowly: `D A D` / `G D G` / `D` / `Dm G` / `D G` / `A` / `D A D G D` — one downstroke per chord |
| 3 | Play along quietly with the recording for verses 1–2 only; freeze on wrong chords, fix, continue |
| 1 | Log: which switch was messiest + clean-switch count on D↔A |

**Week-1 success look:** you can play the Verse-1 chord loop at a slow steady tempo without stopping to rebuild each shape. Singing can wait until switches are automatic.
